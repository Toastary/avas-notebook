## Relevance
Does the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) authorize the President to impose tariffs? President [[Research/Library/People/Donald Trump\|Donald Trump]] invoked IEEPA to increase tariffs on US imports, we've seen these tariffs gone from 25%-100%. Did the statute grant the authority over tariffs to the [[Research/Library/Goverment/Executive\|Executive]]? Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 grants Congress the authority to impose taxes, tariffs are a tax on imports. This court case reinterpreted the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), 50 U.S.C. § 1701 doesn't grant the authority to impose tariffs.

## Major Points of Dissent
Associate Justice [[Research/Library/People/Kavanaugh\|Kavanaugh]] gave a dissenting opinion in which he clarified that there are statutes in place that grants the President the ability to impose tariffs lawfully. He pointed out laws such as the [[Research/Laws/United States/Federal Laws/Tariff Act of 1930\|Tariff Act of 1930]], and [[Research/Laws/United States/Federal Laws/Trade Expansion Act of 1962\|Trade Expansion Act of 1962]]. When it comes to Section 338 of the [[Research/Laws/United States/Federal Laws/Tariff Act of 1930\|Tariff Act of 1930]] looking at clause (2) The country has to be found discriminating the commerce of the [[Research/Library/Geography/United States\|United States]], directly or indirectly.
