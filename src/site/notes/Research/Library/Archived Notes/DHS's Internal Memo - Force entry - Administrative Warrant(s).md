## Source
[Form I-205](https://www.ice.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Document/2017/I-205_SAMPLE.PDF) | Accessed Friday, May 15, 2026
	This form isn't signed by a judge it is typically signed by a supervisor, ICE, or immigration judges (under the executive and not under the judicial branch)

DHS memo: [Anonymous whistleblower disclosure](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/26499371-dhs-ice-memo-1-21-26/)
1. This policy directive authorizes Immigration and Customs Enforcement ("ICE") Agents to enter homes and residences without consent, in order to conduct arrests and removals without a warrant issued by a federal judge

Page 2:
1. a. By memorandum dated May 12, 2025 ("the May 12 Memo"), Acting ICE Director [[Research/Library/People/Todd Lyons\|Todd Lyons]] authorizes ICE agents to "forcibly enter into certain people's homes without a judicial warrant", consent, or an emergency

Page 3:
1. The Congressional Research Service ("CRS") reviewed and analyzed ICE's legal authorities. Notably, CRS found that ICE warrants do not confer the same authority as judicial warrants 

## Notes
This memorandum justifies the usage of administrative warrant(s) typically form I-905. Whether this violates the 4th amendment or not is up to the court(s) interpretation but as written in the 4th amendment the warrant must be signed by a judge which is typically a judicial warrant. This memorandum justifies the force entry with just an administrative warrant to initiate a final removal (all according to the whistleblower).

I-205 isn't unconstitutional it is an actual warrant that can be used for removal(s)/deportation(s)
